Overview

LLMs can generate multiple plausible reasoning paths for the same query, making reliable response selection an important part of test-time scaling. Majority voting is limited to answers that can be normalized and aggregated, while learned verifiers require additional labeled data and training.

ReFeri recycles the few-shot examples already available in the prompt as a training-free verifier. The same demonstrations used to guide generation are reused to evaluate which candidate best follows the intended task while avoiding demonstration-specific shortcuts.

ReFeri is motivated by a Bayes-inspired decomposition of candidate likelihood into two complementary terms. A forward confidence score measures how well a candidate follows the given few-shot guidance. A backward reconstruction penalty measures how strongly the candidate helps reconstruct the demonstrations, which can expose excessive dependence on demonstration-specific patterns.

Training-free No task-specific reward model or verifier training is required.
Few-shot recycling Demonstrations are used twice: first for generation, then again for verification.
Test-time scaling The selector remains effective as the candidate pool grows.

Main Results

Across three generation LLMs and seven reasoning benchmarks, ReFeri consistently improves training-free response selection. Relative to Few-shot CoT with random candidate selection, the paper reports an 8.2% average relative gain; compared with the strongest prior training-free baseline, the average relative improvement is 2.6%.

Test-time Scaling

ReFeri scales reliably as the number of candidate responses increases. With GPT-4o-mini generation, accuracy increases from 75.8 → 79.4 on MATH500, 41.4 → 45.5 on GPQA, and 75.6 → 86.0 on MuSR-ta when increasing the candidate pool from one to twenty responses.

Figure 2. Accuracy as the candidate pool grows. ReFeri continues to select higher-quality responses, while several other training-free selectors saturate or degrade.

What Does the Backward Term Capture?

The backward term is not intended as a standalone correctness measure. Instead, it is interpreted jointly with the forward score. The analyses below show that a large backward term is particularly sensitive to candidates that reconstruct or mimic demonstration-specific structure.

Figure 3. Candidates with larger raw backward terms are better at reconstructing the original few-shot answers, consistent with the demonstration-dependence that ReFeri is designed to suppress.
Figure 4. Prepending demonstration fragments increases the backward term much more sharply than the forward score, directly testing its sensitivity to demonstration artifacts.
  • The backward term provides a signal that is complementary to ordinary forward likelihood.
  • Subtracting it helps avoid selecting candidates that receive high scores by copying demonstration-specific structure.
  • The effect is asymmetric: the penalty is most useful when the incorrect candidate is more demonstration-similar.
